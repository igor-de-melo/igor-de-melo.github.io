### Competition rockets at LASC

TOPUS, Latin American Space Challenge · 2020 – 2022

**Problem:** Design competition rockets that reach their category’s target apogee and fly safely.

**Approach:** I worked on propulsion and flight mechanics: **6-DOF** trajectory simulation to verify performance and flight conditions, and **Monte Carlo** runs to map ballistic and non-ballistic impact regions for the safety analysis.

**Result:** **First place** in all three editions (table below).

Stack: MATLAB, 6-DOF simulation, Monte Carlo method, CFD

- **2022, Rigel (1 km), motor (team work):** K-class solid motor, fine **KNSB** propellant in 3 grains of 150 mm (Ø 65.7 mm, 33 mm core), Ø 69.85 mm × 502 mm chamber, 1.93 kg of propellant. One motor tested per project.
- **2022, calculated values** (Nakka internal ballistics spreadsheet): average thrust 1,212 N, peak 1,410 N, total impulse **2,418 N·s**, specific impulse 127.8 s, burn time of about 1.9 s, maximum chamber pressure 4.74 MPa.
- **2022, flight simulated in MATLAB:** wind, 3 m launch rail, 84° elevation and two-stage recovery (0.43 m² drogue; 2.81 m² main, deployed at 480 m). Simulated: apogee of 1,160 m, maximum velocity of 166 m/s, descent at 8.6 m/s under the main. **Apogee reached: 1,168 m** (source: [Portal USP São Carlos](https://saocarlos.usp.br/topus-conquista-dois-titulos-na-latin-america-space-challenge-2022/), in Portuguese).
- **2022, Monte Carlo:** **5,000 simulations** with dispersions of 10% in thrust, 25% in drag coefficient, 50% in wind speed and 15° in wind direction, to estimate the landing area; simulated mean apogee of 1,153 m, range of about 920 to 1,420 m.
- **2021, Rigel (1 km):** simulated apogee of 1,001 m and simulated maximum velocity of 155 m/s, also with Monte Carlo.
- **2020, Curupira (500 m):** **1st place** in the Entry-Level 0.5 km category (8th overall).
